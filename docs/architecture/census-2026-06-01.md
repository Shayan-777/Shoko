# Architecture Census — 2026-06-01

A grounded, measured snapshot of the codebase, taken to decide *where* refactoring
effort should and should not go. Every number here is measured from `lib/`, not estimated.

## Verdict in one line

**The macro-architecture is essentially correct and well-enforced. The pain is
concentrated, intra-adapter over-decomposition — not a hexagonal problem.**

The feeling of "I fix one thing and the previous fix stops making sense" is not
coming from the layers. It is coming from carving classes into single-use modules
with no fixed rule for when to stop, plus a guardrail suite that has grown into a
fossil record of past intentions.

## What is HEALTHY — do not spend effort here

1. **The dependency rule holds almost perfectly.**
   - `core/` → outer layers: **0** real references (2 hits are comments).
   - `application/` → concrete `Adapters::`: **0**.
   - `application/` → `Composition::`: **0**.
   - Nothing requires adapters back into `core/`/`application/`.
   - Adapters call into application only through the inbound direction (11 refs to
     `Application::Services`/`UseCases`/`Workflows`).
   This is the single most important law of hexagonal architecture, and it is enforced.

2. **Ports are real and correctly placed** in `application/ports/{inbound,outbound}`
   (80 port modules).

3. **File sizes are fine.** Only 15 files exceed 400 LOC; only 19 are under 15 LOC.
   The bulk sit in the healthy 16–200 LOC band. *File size is not the problem* — do
   not go on a splitting or merging spree based on length.

## The THREE real problems (in priority order)

### P1 — Single-use mixin shredding (~146 modules) — *the churn engine*
- 226 modules are `include`d in exactly one place. 80 are ports (legitimate).
  That leaves **~146 single-use mixins**: private behavior carved out of one host
  class into its own file, included once.
- Examples: `UiControllerPopupActions` (92 LOC, included once into `UiController`),
  `AnnotationDispatch` (91), `BrowseScreenComponentListRenderer` (204),
  `DependencyAccess` (46), the `PortAndRepositoryRegistration*` shards of the
  composition root.
- Why this is *the* problem: a module included exactly once gives you the
  file-count and indirection of decomposition with **none** of its benefits — no
  narrowed interface, no isolated test seam, shared private state with the host.
  Because there is no principled line for "extract vs. inline," every refactoring
  pass re-litigates the last one. This is precisely what the recent commits show:
  *split into `*_support` mixins → fold 28 back → fold the rest → rename twice.*
- Concentrated in `adapters/ui` (162 files), `adapters/input` (113), and the
  composition root.
- NOTE: a single-use *collaborator object* (a real dependency the host calls) is
  fine. The smell is specifically the **`include`-once module** form.

### P2 — Deep nesting + climbing requires — *the maze feeling*
- Directory depth: 282 files at depth 5, 108 at depth 6, 13 at depth 7, 4 at depth 8.
  Over 400 files sit at depth ≥5.
- **623 `require_relative` statements climb 3+ levels** (`../../../…`) to reach siblings.
- Worst offender: the composition root, e.g.
  `container_factory/controller_composition/reader_runtime_assembler/controller_builder/ui_graph_builder/controller_assembly/dictionary_builder.rb`
  — 8 levels of builders/assemblers. A composition root should be boring and flat.
- Largely *caused by* P1: each shred earns its own subdirectory.

### P3 — Guardrail over-growth (57 specs / 4,394 LOC) — *the fossil record*
- 57 architecture spec files, 162 examples. Successive moods each left a layer:
  `hexagonal_migration`, `hexagonal_hardening`, `hexagonal_coherence`,
  `final_hardening` — four specs encoding the *same* concern at four different times.
- Heavy redundancy:
  - **~9 rescue/error micro-specs** (`no_standard_error_rescue`, `no_swallowing_rescue`,
    `no_rescue_exception`, `no_bare_string_raise`, `no_noop_reraise_rescue`,
    `no_overlapping_rescue_chains`, `no_rescue_numeric_default`,
    `no_rescue_literal_default`, `no_proc_type_fallbacks`) — one example each. These
    are one "rescue conventions" spec.
  - **~9 overlapping hexagonal/boundary specs** (`adapter_boundary`, `layer_dependency`,
    `layer_policy_consistency`, `strict_hexagonal_wiring`, `no_cross_adapter_runtime_coupling`,
    plus the four hexagonal_* above).
  - Migration fossils: `no_legacy_artifacts` (312 LOC), `no_legacy_factory_keys`,
    `session_wrapper_removal`.
- The suite *feels* like binding law, but half of it is sediment that re-opens
  settled questions and makes every change feel constrained in conflicting directions.

## Implication for the constitution

The rules that will actually stop the churn, derived from the evidence above:

1. **Ban the include-once mixin.** Private behavior stays as private methods on the
   host. Extract only into a *named collaborator object* with its own reason to exist
   and its own test. (Targets ~146 modules — the biggest lever.)
2. **Cap adapter nesting** (e.g., ≤4 levels under `lib/shoko/adapters`) and flatten
   the composition root. Kills most of the 623 climbing requires.
3. **Do NOT use file length as a refactoring trigger.** Sizes are healthy.
4. **Consolidate the guardrail suite** from 57 specs to a small coherent set
   (~8–10), deleting fossils and merging redundant clusters. The kept specs *are*
   the constitution, executable.
5. **The boundaries are done.** No further "hexagonal hardening" work.

## Stopping rule (for the whole effort)

Done = consolidated guardrails green + zero constitution violations on a single
inside-out pass. *Not* "feels clean." The feeling never converges; the checklist does.
